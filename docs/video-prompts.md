# Video prompts: every place in the game that wants motion

Generated by `npm run video:prompts`. Do not hand-edit.

**6 clips.** Every prompt carries the same STYLE anchor as the card
art, because motion that does not match the cards reads as glued on.

## Six clips, and why only six

This file used to ask for 72, across gods, astras, endings and battle beats.
It should not have, and the reason is written at the top of its own generator:
motion that does not match the cards reads as glued on.

Since that was written, every one of those subjects got a PAINTING. 129 cards
and 8 deities, all shipped. A generated clip of Agni will not be our Agni, and
a generated Brahmastra will not be the Brahmastra the player is holding. It is
guaranteed to mismatch the thing it sits next to.

Those subjects are also the ones AI video is worst at: a specific figure with
an identity to hold for five seconds, rather than weather. So they are built in
code instead, out of the paintings we already own. The card art itself is what
blooms, shakes and settles when an astra fires, which cannot mismatch the card
because it IS the card.

What is left is the one thing code cannot fake and video is genuinely good at:
drifting atmosphere with no object in it.

## Before generating anything

**Composite mode matters more than the prompt.** Veo will not give you an
alpha channel, so anything that sits *over* the board has to be generated on
pure black and composited with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, which drops the black
and keeps the light. That is why every fire, lightning and light effect below
says "on black". Darkening effects (ash, withering, scorch) are the reverse:
generate on white, composite with `multiply`.

| comp | how it is used | generate on |
|---|---|---|
| `background` | full-bleed behind a screen | any ground |
| `screen` | glow/fire/light over the board | **pure black** |
| `multiply` | ash/shadow/scorch over the board | **white** |
| `card` | inside a card frame | any ground |

**Loops will not loop.** Veo does not produce seamless loops. For the six
ambient clips, pick slow drifting motion where the first and last frames are
similar, then run two `<video>` elements offset by half the duration and
crossfade. A hard cut is visible on fast motion and invisible on drifting dust.

**Sizes: 720p, not 480p.** That advice was written when this file asked for
72 clips and the whole set had to fit in roughly 15 MB. It asks for six now,
and the art already shipped is 7.6 MB, so there is room. These are full-bleed
backgrounds: at 480p upscaled to a phone they go soft, and soft is the one
thing a hand-painted look cannot survive. WebM/VP9 first with an MP4/H.264
fallback for Safari. Low frame rates are fine and often better; drifting dust
at 12-15 fps reads as hand-painted rather than cheap.

**Portrait.** The game is mobile-first. The six ambient backgrounds want a 9:16
variant as well as 16:9; everything else can be centre-safe 16:9.

**Order of work.** All six. That is the whole list.

6 clips, 48s of footage.

### `ambient-menu-dawn`

**Where:** Main menu, behind the title  
**Length:** 8s · LOOPS · composite `background`

```
Ink-and-gouache fantasy illustration, bold black linework, opaque gouache brushwork, muted earthy palette, dramatic chiaroscuro. The empty field of Kurukshetra at first light, before either army has formed up. Tall dry grass moving in a slow wind, banners on distant poles stirring, thin dust drifting low across the ground. No people. Wide, still, the horizon low in frame. Almost nothing happens. Hand-painted animation feel, subtle grain, no camera fly-through, no 3D render, no lens flare, no photorealism. Slow and deliberate.
```
